## What Should You Look For in a Crypto Trading Platform?

Before comparing fees or counting listed coins, it helps to define your use case. A platform for occasional Bitcoin purchases has different requirements from one used for high-frequency futures trading.

### 1. Trading costs

Trading fees are only one part of the cost. You should also consider:

- Maker and taker fees
- Bid-ask spread
- Funding fees on perpetual futures
- Withdrawal fees
- Deposit fees
- Network fees
- Conversion costs
- Slippage during fast markets

A platform advertising a low headline fee may still be expensive if the spread is wide or the order is filled at a poor price. On OKX, the fee shown before placing an order depends on the account’s fee tier and the specific trading pair. The final fee can also vary if an order fills in multiple parts or receives maker and taker treatment in different circumstances.

### 2. Available markets

A platform may offer hundreds of assets but still lack the particular trading pair you need. Check:

- Whether the asset is available in your jurisdiction
- Whether it trades against USDT, USDC, USD, or another currency
- Whether the platform supports spot, margin, futures, or options for that asset
- How much liquidity the pair has
- Whether the market is subject to special rules

OKX groups spot and futures markets into different fee categories. Its published framework also notes that some pairs may not be available in every jurisdiction.

### 3. Order types and execution tools

Basic market and limit orders may be enough for a first purchase. More active traders may need:

- Stop-loss and take-profit orders
- Trigger orders
- Trailing stops
- Conditional orders
- Grid bots
- Recurring strategies
- API access
- Portfolio or risk controls

The tools are useful only if you understand what they do. A bot can automate a strategy, but it cannot turn a flawed strategy into a sensible one.

### 4. Security and account protection

Look for:

- Two-factor authentication
- Withdrawal controls
- Device and login monitoring
- Whitelisting options
- Proof-of-reserves information
- Clear risk disclosures
- A transparent account recovery process

OKX publishes proof-of-reserves information using zk-STARK-based verification and provides data intended to let users examine reserve ratios and wallet ownership. The published material also makes clear that reserve reports are snapshots and do not cover every possible asset or transaction outside the report scope.

Proof of reserves is useful information, but it is not a guarantee against every operational, market, regulatory, or account-security risk.

## OKX Trading Features Explained

### Spot trading

Spot trading is the most straightforward starting point. You buy or sell the underlying digital asset without borrowing funds or opening a leveraged derivatives position.

It is generally easier to understand than futures because:

- There is no liquidation from leverage
- You hold the purchased asset directly in the exchange account
- The fee calculation is more intuitive
- You can use limit orders to control the maximum purchase or minimum sale price

That does not mean spot trading is risk-free. Crypto prices can move sharply, and the asset itself can lose value.

### Futures and perpetual contracts

Futures and perpetual contracts allow traders to speculate on price movements using margin. Leverage can increase the size of a position relative to the amount deposited, which also magnifies losses.

Common costs may include:

- Opening and closing trading fees
- Funding payments between long and short traders
- Liquidation-related costs
- Spread and slippage
- Borrowing or margin-related charges, depending on the product

A lower futures trading fee does not make leverage safer. If you are new to crypto, learn how margin, maintenance margin, liquidation price, and funding work before using a live position.

### Copy trading

OKX supports spot and futures copy trading. Users can browse lead-trader profiles and configure settings such as fixed copy amounts, proportional copy amounts, maximum total allocation, and supported assets. The platform states that spot copy trading does not use leverage and does not involve liquidation risk in the same way as futures copy trading.

However, “copy” does not mean “guaranteed.” A lead trader can lose money, change strategy, take larger risks than expected, or perform well during one market period and poorly during another.

Before following a trader, examine:

- Maximum drawdown
- Trading history length
- Average position size
- Use of leverage
- Number of active trades
- Whether returns came from a short period
- How much capital you are willing to allocate

Treat copy trading as an automated execution method, not as a replacement for risk management.

### Trading bots

OKX Smart Trading includes bot trading, copy trading, and signal-related tools. The platform positions these features as ways to automate or replicate trading activity.

Depending on the product, bots may help with:

- Grid strategies
- Recurring purchases
- Portfolio rebalancing
- Rule-based entries and exits
- Automated orders within a defined price range

Bots work best when the market conditions match the strategy. A grid bot designed for a sideways market can perform poorly during a strong breakout or sudden collapse. Set a maximum allocation, define when to stop the strategy, and avoid placing your entire portfolio into one automated system.
